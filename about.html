About AIM Lab

Lab Overview

The AeroAstro Intelligent Mechanics Lab (AIM Lab) at New Mexico State University (NMSU) is led by Prof. Sungmoon Choi. Our research spans the full spectrum of aerospace systems, machine learning, and space mechanics—encompassing fundamental mathematical theories, theoretical formulations, algorithmic and simulation developments, and beyond.

By bridging fundamental celestial mechanics and aerospace engineering with state-of-the-art artificial intelligence, optimization techniques, and mathematical foundations, AIM Lab addresses challenging open problems in deep-space flight mechanics, trajectory design, space navigation, and multidisciplinary aerospace system engineering.

Core Research Interests

  • Astrodynamics & Celestial Mechanics: Multi-body orbital dynamics and chaotic motion.
  • Space Mission Design & Trajectory Optimization: Interplanetary trajectory design, gravity-assist sequencing, moon tour problems, and aerobraking.
  • Orbit Determination & Deep-Space Navigation: Angles-only optical navigation, initial orbit determination, state estimation, and autonomous spacecraft navigation.
  • Spacecraft Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC): Real-time trajectory generation, robust feedback control, and autonomous maneuver planning.
  • Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning: Reinforcement Learning, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), Extreme Theory of Functional Connections (XTFC), Diffusion Models, and deep learning approximations for orbital mechanics and aerospace systems.

🚀 Prospective Students

AIM Lab is actively looking for motivated Ph.D., M.S., and Undergraduate research students who are passionate about astrodynamics, space mission design, space navigation, and AI/ML applications in aerospace engineering.

Strong candidates typically possess a solid background in applied mathematics, astrodynamics, and machine learning, or related engineering disciplines, along with proficiency in programming (e.g., Python, MATLAB, Julia).
