About AIM Lab

Lab Overview

The AeroAstro Intelligent Mechanics Lab (AIM Lab) at New Mexico State University (NMSU) is led by Prof. Sungmoon Choi. Our laboratory focuses on pioneering theoretical, computational, and algorithmic frameworks for next-generation space exploration and autonomous aerospace systems.

By combining rigorous celestial mechanics with cutting-edge artificial intelligence, machine learning, and optimization techniques, AIM Lab addresses challenging open problems in deep-space flight mechanics, optical navigation, trajectory design, and multidisciplinary space system engineering.

Core Research Interests

  • Astrodynamics & Celestial Mechanics: Multi-body orbital dynamics, libration point orbits, and low-thrust trajectory mechanics.
  • Space Mission Design & Trajectory Optimization: Interplanetary trajectory design, gravity-assist sequencing, low-thrust trajectory optimization, and aerobraking.
  • Orbit Determination & Deep-Space Navigation: Angles-only optical navigation, initial orbit determination without prior attitude/distance data, and autonomous spacecraft navigation.
  • Spacecraft Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC): Real-time trajectory generation, robust feedback control, and autonomous maneuver placement.
  • Physics-Informed Machine Learning & AI: Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), Extreme Theory of Functional Connections (XTFC), and deep learning approximations for orbital mechanics problems.

🚀 Prospective Students

AIM Lab is actively looking for motivated Ph.D., M.S., and Undergraduate research students who are passionate about astrodynamics, space mission design, optical navigation, and AI/ML applications in aerospace engineering.

Strong candidates typically possess a solid background in aerospace engineering, mechanical engineering, computer science, physics, applied mathematics, or related engineering disciplines, along with proficiency in programming (e.g., Python, MATLAB, C/C++).
